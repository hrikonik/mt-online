Switch challenge

You'll see a row of symbols and an operator (funnel). Work out what the operator does to the row, then pick the matching description. 6 minutes, unlimited questions, adaptive difficulty.

Row 1 passes through the operator below and becomes Row 2. Which rule explains the change?

+ Show operator reference
  • Reverse — the row's order is flipped end-to-end.
  • Rotate — every symbol shifts one position, wrapping around.
  • Swap ends — only the first and last symbols trade places.
  • Mirror pair — the two symbols in the middle swap.
  • Sort by size — symbols are reordered smallest to largest.

Grid challenge

Memorize a pattern of highlighted cells, answer one interference puzzle, then click the cells you memorized. Both halves are scored — this is a dual-task working-memory test, not just memory.

Memorize
+ Show strategy notes
  • Chunk the lit cells into a shape or path rather than memorizing coordinates one by one.
  • Scan in a fixed order every round (e.g. left-to-right, top-to-bottom) so encoding stays consistent under pressure.
  • Give the distractor puzzle full attention — it's scored independently of recall, so rushing it costs points without protecting your memory.
  • If you lose the exact pattern, recall the general region rather than guessing at random — partial credit beats a wild guess.

Digit challenge

Fill in the missing digit so the equation holds true. Elementary arithmetic only — the constraint is speed, not difficulty. 6 minutes, ~8 seconds per question.

Session complete

+ Show strategy notes
  • Use elimination on the missing digit rather than solving algebraically — test the most likely candidates first.
  • Refresh times tables and complements to 10/20/100 — recall speed matters more than raw calculation ability.
  • If a question is taking too long, submit your best guess and move on — one slow question costs more than one wrong guess.
